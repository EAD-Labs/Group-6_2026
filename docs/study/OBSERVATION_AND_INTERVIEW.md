## Neutral facilitation

Say: "We are testing the application, not you. Use the instructions as you normally would. Tell us what you expect to happen. You can pause or stop."

Do not demonstrate the right path or answer before the baseline. If the participant is stuck, first ask "What are you trying to do?"; after a reasonable agreed wait, offer the least assistance that permits progress. Record the time, exact help type and whether it resolves the issue. Stop immediately for discomfort, consent withdrawal or a data/privacy concern.

Help codes: **none**, **clarification** (restate task), **navigation** (point to a control), **procedure** (explain sequence), **concept** (explain source/prompt principle), **technical** (restore access/network), **peer** (another participant). Use multiple rows when help changes.

Distinguish an interface error (could not find Next) from a conceptual error (did not know why the claim was unsupported). Neither should be silently inferred from a completion checkbox. For elapsed time, retain interruptions and technical downtime separately. Do not delete slow or unfinished participants from the analysis.

## Short interview - 10 minutes

1. What did you expect this control or step to do?
2. Show how you would find evidence for this sentence before using it in class.
3. What changed in your second request, and why?
4. Which part needed the most help? What would make it clearer?
5. What would you check or change for your own learners?
6. What would help you return for another short session?
7. Is there anything about the privacy, saved work or AI explanation that is unclear?

Use open questions and listen without defending the design. Do not ask "Did this save time?" as if that outcome were expected. If a participant volunteers a benefit, record it as perception with context, not measured time saving.
