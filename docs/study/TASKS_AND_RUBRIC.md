# Parallel tasks and scoring guide

Original fictional teaching material, created for this exploratory project. The four-dimension rubric is project-created and not an externally validated instrument. Do not use it for employment, ranking teachers or awarding course completion.

## Administration

Choose task order before sessions: odd study IDs receive A at baseline/B at exit; even IDs B/A. Record any deviation. Keep the source/draft difficulty similar; the two tasks are not proven equivalent. Do not show answer guidance during baseline or post-task. Offer the same neutral instruction and time allowance. Record every assistance prompt.

Participant instruction: **"Use these notes to request a short Class 6 teaching activity with one learning check. Review the supplied draft, identify unsupported material, and revise it. Say what you would check before using it with learners."** Allow 10 minutes per task; stopping early is permitted. Do not judge by typing speed.

## Task A - evaporation

Source notes:

> Water in an open dish can slowly change into water vapour. This change is called evaporation. The speed depends on conditions, including temperature and exposed surface area. In a classroom observation, compare equal amounts of water in identical dishes. Record the conditions and the change over time. A single observation has limits; repeat and compare before drawing a general conclusion.

Draft to inspect:

> Put equal amounts of water in two dishes and record changes. Evaporation only occurs when water boils. A dish that loses water once proves that heat is always the only cause. Ask: "What observations would you record, and why should we repeat the comparison?"

Observer-only guidance: "only when water boils" is not supported by the supplied notes and is incorrect; "heat is always the only cause" overclaims beyond one observation and ignores other conditions. A defensible revision describes slow vapour change, comparable conditions and repeated observation. Do not require these exact words. Ask the teacher to cite the relevant passage rather than reward memorised terminology.

## Task B - shadows

Source notes:

> A shadow forms when an opaque object blocks light from reaching a surface. Its size and position can change when the object, light source or surface moves. In a classroom comparison, use the same object and surface; change one position at a time and record what happens. Repeat each comparison. An observation supports a conclusion about those conditions; it does not prove every light source will produce the same result.

Draft to inspect:

> Move a torch and record the shadow of the same object on the same surface. Shadows have one fixed size wherever the light source is placed. One observation proves that all lights make identical shadows. Ask: "What did you change, and what should stay the same in your next comparison?"

Observer-only guidance: fixed size and identical shadows overclaim against the supplied notes. A defensible revision identifies blocking light, records size/position, changes one condition and repeats. Check source linkage and learner task quality rather than exact phrasing.

## Rubric: four dimensions, 0-2 each

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Task/audience | Purpose and audience absent/unusable | Either purpose/audience partly specified; activity unclear | Clear Class 6 purpose and an actionable task |
| Source boundary | Unsupported claims accepted or added without checking | Notices a problem but correction/source unclear | Identifies a specific unsupported claim and makes a passage-supported correction |
| Learning check | Missing or unrelated | Generic recall or partially aligned check | Answerable check aligned with observation/evidence purpose |
| Teacher review | Accepts draft without review | Vague "check it" statement | States specific evidence/learner/privacy review and a concrete revision |

Record each dimension independently before calculating total out of 8. Use **not observed** when the opportunity was absent; do not replace it with zero. Retain the output/evidence needed to explain a rating only with participant permission and approved storage. Log whether assistance changed the response.

Two reviewers should independently rate at least three paired records where available. Preserve first ratings; calculate exact agreement per dimension, discuss differences and store a separate agreed rating with reason. Do not overwrite the first scores or describe a small agreement sample as validation.

## Other observed application tasks

| ID | Neutral task | Observable completion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L1 | Find a short lesson, explain its next action and answer its check | Finds route and operates check; conceptual explanation noted separately |
| C1 | Improve a prompt and compare checks | Edits a weak dimension and explains change; score increase alone is insufficient |
| S1 | Check a draft claim against its source, edit and mark review | Cites a passage, notices unsupported material and makes a justified edit |
| R1 | Save/leave/return or export a local review | Understands saved/local distinction; resumes own work without another account's data |
| H1 | In self-paced course, test/repair/retest a helper | Actual specification versions and six v1/three same-input v2 evidence; not completed in the initial short session by assumption |

Course quiz scores and this observational rubric are separate measures. A baseline/post change may reflect practice, assistance, differing task difficulty or prior experience; it does not isolate a platform effect.
