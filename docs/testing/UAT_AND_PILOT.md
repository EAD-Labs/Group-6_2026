# User acceptance and ten-participant pilot plan

Prepared 1 October 2026 against HLD v1.3, section 14. **Planned; not executed or signed off.** Local automated evidence supports development review. It does not replace acceptance in the approved deployment, participant observation, a measured load test or a field study.

## Test record and entry conditions

Record the release revision, URL, Supabase project, migration versions, browser/OS/device, network profile, test account role, date, tester and evidence path for every run. Use synthetic accounts and classroom sources. Prepare participants A and B, an administrator, a facilitator assigned to cohort X, a second cohort Y, a content manager, an incomplete learner and a legitimately completed test learner. Do not fabricate passing progress through a public API to simplify certificate testing.

Before external UAT: owner restores authorised deployment access; staging credentials and migrations are verified; safe-use/privacy wording and provider terms are approved; an operator and facilitator are available; account recovery works; automated checks pass; no known critical data/privacy defect remains. Current blockers are missing local connected-service configuration and Vercel team access (403). Record actual service outcomes after these are resolved.

Result vocabulary: **Pass** (observed with evidence), **Fail** (expected result not observed), **Blocked** (dependency prevents execution), **Not run**, or **Accepted change** (named decision and approver; not a disguised pass). The nineteen cases below start **Not run**. Where the current scope differs from the HLD, the reviewer must resolve that difference explicitly.

## Ten-participant pilot facilitation

Purpose: identify where beginner teachers cannot complete the core tasks independently, whether they can improve a prompt and verify a source-supported resource, and what support they need. Ten people form an exploratory pilot; do not infer population-wide effectiveness or causal learning gains from this sample.

Recruit ten teachers/educators matching the intended beginner audience. Record relevant teaching context and device/access needs only when needed for interpretation. Offer a concise information sheet stating purpose, activity duration, what is collected, who sees it, retention, withdrawal contact and the voluntary nature of study participation. Keep research consent separate from course safe-use acknowledgement. Do not require real pupil records or identifiable student work. Do not record screens/audio without specific permission.

Assign pseudonymous study IDs P01–P10. Keep any identity key separately with limited access. Use the approved cohort for operational tracking; use a separate de-identified analysis sheet for observations. Schedule ten individual slots or two groups of five with a facilitator and observer. Group help must be logged because it affects independent-task interpretation.

### Proposed schedule

| Stage | Activity | Evidence and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before invitation | Complete release/privacy/accessibility checks; rehearse all tasks with synthetic accounts; verify recovery and export/delete. | Facilitator dry-run and approved information sheet; do not recruit into a known data-risk defect. |
| First 75-minute session | 10m introduction/consent/device check; 10m baseline tasks; 15m one lesson and concept check; 15m CRAFT revision/comparison; 15m source claim audit and edit; 10m export/resume and debrief. | This is a usability sample of the course, not completion of all 29 lessons. Record help, failures and time per task. |
| Self-paced course period | Offer short sessions across the agreed field-study window; complete the four modules, Staffroom repair/retest and guided Source Studio portfolio. Provide a scheduled support contact. | Dates and attendance are planned until observed. Record actual return/completion, not estimates as learning time. |
| Exit session | Parallel post-task, brief interview, review participant export and missing requirements, certificate test only for genuine eligibility. | Score with the same rubric; report individual changes and limitations. An uncompleted participant is not silently removed from the sample. |
| Team review | Consolidate defects, pedagogical difficulties, support needs and priority changes. Retest fixes with permission. | De-identified findings, defect owners and client decision. No pilot outcome entered before collection. |

Use one original fictional science passage for baseline and a different but similarly demanding passage for the exit task. Example baseline task: “Ask for a Class 4 explanation using the supplied passage and a three-question check. Identify one unsupported claim in the draft and revise it.” Do not show the model answer during baseline. Counterbalance task order where practical and record unequal assistance. This is a small study design choice, not a validated assessment instrument.

Score four dimensions 0–2 each: **task/audience** (missing / partly specified / clear and useful); **source boundary** (unsupported material accepted / issue noticed / specific passage-based correction); **learning check** (absent / generic / aligned and answerable); **teacher review** (accepts without review / vague check / stated evidence and revision). Use two reviewers on a sample, discuss disagreements and retain the original scores. Report the rubric as project-created; do not present it as an externally validated scale.

### Observer sheet and interview

For each task record study ID, device/browser, start/end, completed independently/with help/not completed, help type, observed mistake, recovery, and one direct quotation only with consent. Keep “could not find the next lesson” separate from “could not explain why a claim was unsupported.” Do not store raw source documents or entire prompts by default.

Ask: “What did you expect this control to do?”, “What would you check before using this with pupils?”, “Show how you would find the evidence for this sentence”, “What changed in your second prompt and why?”, and “What would help you return for the next session?” Invite criticism without explaining away a confusing interface. Do not ask participants to endorse a time-saving claim.

Summarise the denominator for every result: invited, consented, started, returned, completed, withdrew and missing observations. Report task completion, help required, typical time with spread, recurring errors and qualitative themes. Report pre/post rubric changes descriptively alongside prior experience and assistance; the design does not isolate a platform effect. Quotes must be de-identified. Agree retention and deletion dates for the study data with the owner.

## Defects, exit and sign-off

Log each issue with case ID, environment/revision, expected/actual result, reproducible steps, severity, evidence, owner, due date and retest result. Critical means a privacy/security breach, data loss or inability to complete a primary journey. High means a major failure without an acceptable workaround. Do not release with an unresolved Critical; resolve High findings or obtain the specific written client decision required by the HLD, subject to the agreed security gate.

Acceptance record: release; test date; cases passed/failed/blocked/not run; agreed scope changes; open issues and disposition; accessibility/performance/privacy review links; three delivery-report links; reviewer name; decision; decision date. Leave all sign-off fields blank until an authorised reviewer actually decides. Pilot report record: actual dates/sample, procedure deviations, consent/data handling, collected observations, findings, limits and recommendations. **No participant results or sign-off are claimed in this plan.**
