# PromptShala Participant Curriculum

## Purpose and audience

PromptShala is a short, practice-first AI literacy pathway for beginner school teachers and other educators. It does not attempt to teach machine-learning mathematics or software development. It helps a teacher use generative AI safely for real classroom preparation while retaining professional judgment.

The four modules follow the product flow defined in the HLD: learn a small concept, complete an interaction, practise a classroom task, receive visible feedback, pass a short quiz, and unlock the next module.

## Curriculum principles

- Use plain language and familiar school examples.
- Prefer interaction and practice over long lectures.
- Never require real student names, records, grades, health information, or other personal data.
- Treat AI output as a draft that a teacher must verify and adapt.
- Provide feedback against a visible rubric rather than only marking an answer right or wrong.
- Keep activities usable on both a mobile phone and a desktop browser.
- Allow retrying without penalty and explain why an answer needs improvement.

## Module 1 — AI Foundations and Responsible Use

### Participant goal

Understand what generative AI can and cannot do, identify appropriate classroom uses, and follow a safe teacher-review process.

### Lesson sequence

1. Introductory activity: “AI, automation, or search?”
2. What generative AI does at a high level
3. Useful teacher tasks and common limitations
4. Privacy, bias, hallucinations, and human review
5. Five-question knowledge check

### Measurable learning outcomes

By the end of the module, the participant can:

- classify at least four of five examples as generative AI, search, or rule-based automation;
- identify at least three limitations of generative AI;
- select safe handling for classroom information in at least four of five scenarios;
- describe the teacher’s responsibility to check facts, age suitability, tone, and inclusivity before use; and
- score at least 70% on the module quiz.

### Evidence captured

- activity selections and explanations viewed;
- privacy scenario score;
- one-sentence reflection on a useful teacher task;
- quiz score and retry count.

## Module 2 — Classroom Prompt Writing

### Participant goal

Turn a vague request into a clear, classroom-ready prompt and improve it using structured feedback.

### Lesson sequence

1. Compare a vague prompt with a classroom-ready prompt
2. Learn CRAFT: Context, Role, Action, Format and Target
3. Build a prompt one field at a time
4. Submit a prompt for rubric feedback
5. Revise the prompt using the feedback
6. Practise with teacher-specific scenarios
7. Five-question knowledge check

### Measurable learning outcomes

By the end of the module, the participant can:

- write a prompt that makes Context, Role, Action, Format and Target visible;
- improve the CRAFT structure coverage between a first and revised attempt while retaining safe-use and teacher-review boundaries;
- revise a first attempt and explain at least two improvements;
- produce one usable prompt for a selected classroom scenario; and
- score at least 70% on the module quiz.

### Evidence captured

- first and revised prompt versions;
- dimension-level rubric scores;
- feedback opened and changes made;
- selected classroom scenario and quiz score.

## Module 3 — Reusable Teacher Assistants

### Participant goal

Convert a successful classroom prompt into a reusable Gem-style assistant specification and test it with more than one input.

### Lesson sequence

1. Prompt versus reusable assistant
2. Define persona, task, context, format, boundaries, and review checks
3. Build a reusable assistant specification
4. Test it with two classroom inputs
5. Identify one failure or weakness and revise the specification
6. Five-question knowledge check

### Measurable learning outcomes

By the end of the module, the participant can:

- explain why a repeated teacher task is suitable or unsuitable for a reusable assistant;
- create an assistant specification containing persona, task, context, output format, constraints, and safety boundaries;
- test the specification with at least two different inputs;
- document one observed weakness and one revision; and
- score at least 70% on the module quiz.

### Evidence captured

- assistant purpose and specification;
- two test inputs;
- teacher review notes;
- revised specification and quiz score.

## Module 4 — Working with Teacher-Owned Sources

### Participant goal

Use a source-grounded notebook workflow to transform non-sensitive, teacher-owned learning material into a classroom draft and verify it against the source.

### Lesson sequence

1. What source-grounded tools such as NotebookLM do
2. Choose safe, non-sensitive source material
3. Request a quiz, study guide, worksheet, slide outline, or audio overview
4. Trace important claims back to the source
5. Apply the teacher review checklist
6. Five-question knowledge check and course reflection

### Measurable learning outcomes

By the end of the module, the participant can:

- select a suitable source that contains no personal or confidential information;
- describe the difference between an open-ended AI response and a source-grounded response;
- create at least one classroom draft from an approved source;
- verify at least three important statements against source citations or source passages;
- complete the privacy, accuracy, age-suitability, accessibility, and copyright review checklist; and
- score at least 70% on the module quiz.

### Evidence captured

- source-type selection without uploading the source to PromptShala;
- chosen artifact type;
- verification checklist;
- final reflection and quiz score.

## Completion and unlocking rules

- A lesson is complete after all required interactions are submitted.
- A module quiz passes at 70% or above. In the five-question prototype quiz, four correct answers are required.
- A failed quiz shows answer-specific explanations and returns the participant to relevant lesson sections.
- Retrying is unlimited for the pilot.
- Passing unlocks the next module and records the completion date.
- Course completion requires all four modules and the final review checklist.

## Pilot measurement for 10 users

For the initial ten-user pilot, collect only the minimum learning analytics required to improve the experience:

- module started/completed;
- first-attempt and final quiz scores;
- prompt rubric dimension scores;
- number of retries;
- time-to-completion by module;
- one optional usefulness rating and one optional comment.

Do not collect prompt content containing identifiable student information. Display a reminder before every free-text activity.
